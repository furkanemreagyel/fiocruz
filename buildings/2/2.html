Pavilhão do Relógio

The Clock Pavilion (1904-1905) is the oldest building in the architectural set of Manguinhos, designed to house activities related to the bubonic plague bacillus, such as the preparation of serum or vaccine. Composed of a single floor, the building housed two laboratories, separated by a central module intended for the horse infirmary. The project reveals influences from English architecture, with the most prominent element being a small central tower, where a clock is installed and is still in operation. The Clock Pavilion served as the headquarters of the Casa de Oswaldo Cruz and is now an exhibition space.

The Clock Pavilion embodies a strictly functionalist design influenced by British industrial and railway architecture. Completed in 1904 to address the urgent need for bubonic plague vaccines, its layout prioritized biosafety and hygiene through a "pavilion system" that utilized balconies for natural ventilation and an independent, internal sewage treatment system to neutralize pathogens. The building’s central feature is its landmark clock tower, which anchors a modular interior once divided into specialized laboratories for bacterial culture and integrated infirmaries for the inoculation and bleeding of horses. According to records from UNESCO and the Oswaldo Cruz Foundation, the pavilion's sober brickwork and linear workflow represent a transition into modern scientific architecture, where the spatial organization was engineered specifically to facilitate life-saving serum production.
