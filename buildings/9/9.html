Arthur Neiva Pavilion

The Courses Pavilion, also known as the Arthur Neiva Pavilion (1947-1951), was created by architect Jorge Ferreira with help from Roberto Burle Marx, who worked on the landscaping and designed the tile panel in the auditorium that shows the life cycle of Trypanosoma cruzi, the cause of Chagas disease. Implanted on the edge of the site facing Brasil Avenue, the building stands out mainly for the presence of the imposing tile panel, listed at the municipal level, as it is part of the set of works by Burle Marx in the city of Rio de Janeiro. The building, listed by the State Institute of Cultural Heritage (Inepac), maintains its original use, housing teaching and research activities of the Oswaldo Cruz Institute.
