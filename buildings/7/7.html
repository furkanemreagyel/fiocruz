Henrique Aragão Pavilion

The Henrique Aragão Pavilion, or Yellow Fever Laboratory—LAFA (1954-1960), designed by Roberto Nadalutti, was projected as a laboratory for the preparation of vaccines against yellow fever. For many years, the building has maintained its original use, linked to industrial production for health, but the current assessment is that these activities need to be shifted to new buildings that can meet contemporary safety standards. This perspective of change of use brought the possibility of elaborating a requalification project for the building, currently under preparation, aiming at valuing the institution’s industrial heritage, expanding public access, and offering more spaces for scientific dissemination on campus.

Reference: Nadalutti, R. (1954). Projeto do Pavilhão Henrique Aragão [Architectural photograph]. Arquivo Histórico da Casa de Oswaldo Cruz.

The Henrique Aragão Pavilion, also known as the Yellow Fever Pavilion, is a hallmark of the Carioca School of Modern Architecture, completed in 1960 to modernize the Manguinhos campus. Designed by architect Roberto Nadalutti, the building functions as a "laboratory machine" characterized by its long horizontal volume and iconic V-shaped pilotis with oval cutouts, which provide structural lightness and a sense of movement. According to UNESCO and Fiocruz heritage records, the design is specifically engineered for the tropical climate, utilizing windowless side facades to block intense sunlight and a layout that optimizes the production of yellow fever vaccines. As a state-listed heritage site, the pavilion represents a critical shift from the institution’s early eclectic style to a modernist vocabulary, where structural artistry and functional hygiene are seamlessly integrated.
