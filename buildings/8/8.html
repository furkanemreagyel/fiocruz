Carlos Augusto da Silva Pavilion

The former Central Cafeteria, currently the Carlos Augusto Silva Pavilion (1947-1951), planned to meet the food needs of the campus employees, was built in an area far from the original architectural core due to the concern, during the 1940s, with the physical delimitation of the institution’s land. Of particular note are the brise-soleils that protect the internal spaces from insolation and the tile panel made by the firm Osirarte (as well as the of the Courses Pavilion), by Paulo Rossi Osir, a tile company   created in 1940 to produce the tile panels of the Ministry building of Education and Health in Rio de Janeiro (now Gustavo Capanema Palace). Jorge Ferreira received an Honorable Mention for the design of the building at the I International Architecture Biennial of São Paulo, in 1951. The building was listed by Inepac and partially maintained its use as a restaurant and is also occupied by occupational health services and administrative areas.

Reference: Casa de Oswaldo Cruz. (n.d.). Pavilhão Carlos Augusto da Silva (Antigo Restaurante Central) [Photograph]. Acervo Fiocruz. https://basearch.coc.fiocruz.br/

The Carlos Augusto Silva Pavilion features brise-soleils (sun-breaking screens) protecting from excessive solar radiation, while tile panels by Burle Marx and Paulo Rossi Osir integrated scientific concepts with artistic expression, a distinctive "tropical modernism" responsive to local climate and values, contrasting with European modernism's pure functional abstraction.
